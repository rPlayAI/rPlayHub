rPlayHub Help

Put your iPhone on your Mac — and drive it from there.

rPlayHub shows a connected iPhone's live screen in a window, turns your mouse and trackpad into taps and swipes, and gives you the device's apps, logs, files, and diagnostics alongside it.

What rPlayHub does

Two things, over one USB or Wi‑Fi connection.

rPlayHub is a native macOS app that stands in for Apple's Device Hub. It does two related jobs, and it helps to keep them apart:

  • See the screen. The phone's display arrives as a live video stream and appears in the View Screen area, cropped to the real screen with no black padding.
  • Drive the phone. Clicks become taps and drags become swipes, sent back to the device as it watches. The same window carries a Home control, screenshots, and recording.

Around the screen sit the tools for looking at the device rather than through it: its apps, its live log, its files and crash reports, its configuration profiles, and the identifiers and health numbers Device Hub shows.

How it's built

A small background service (the engine) owns the privileged connection to the phone and publishes the video and a control channel locally; the app is a client of it. You approve the service once — see below — and after that it starts on its own.

Before you start

The same on‑device setup Device Hub needs. Done once, mostly.

  1. Pair and trust the phone with this Mac once over USB — tap Trust on the phone when it asks. This creates the pairing record the connection relies on.
  2. Turn on Developer Mode on the phone (Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Developer Mode). It needs a restart and an on‑device confirmation. Without it the phone offers none of the services rPlayHub uses.
  3. Approve the engine. The first launch asks you to allow rPlayHub's background service in System Settings ▸ General ▸ Login Items & Extensions. Approve it once.
  4. Connect. Attach the phone by cable, or have it on the same Wi‑Fi network, then pick it in the sidebar.
After a restart

iOS discards its Developer Disk Image every time it reboots. rPlayHub re‑mounts it for you automatically once the phone is unlocked — no Xcode and no Device Hub required. If a freshly rebooted phone won't mirror, unlock it and give it a moment.

Requires iOS 27 or later

Live screen viewing needs iOS 27+. On older systems Apple's own Device Hub reports "screen viewing is unsupported" too — the rest of the tools (info, apps, files, console) still work.

The window

Three areas, in the shape of Device Hub.

  • Sidebar (left). Every device this Mac can see, with a search field and an Available section. A filled dot marks the device you're mirroring; the rest are listed so you can switch. iOS version sits right‑aligned on each row.
  • Screen (center). The live picture. While it's showing, the actions that operate on it live in a strip directly beneath it and on right‑click.
  • Inspector (right). Tabs describing the selected device.

While the screen is embedded, the Controls tab is hidden because the same actions are a right‑click away on the screen itself. Choose View ▸ Show Controls to bring the pane back.

View & control

Your Mac's pointer is the phone's finger.

Tap
Click anywhere on the screen.
Swipe / drag
Press and drag; the gesture is sent as it moves.
Home
Return to the home screen — the strip button, or right‑click ▸ Press Home.
Pin on top
Keep the window floating above other apps.
Rotate view
Turn the picture (and the input mapping with it) when you're holding the phone sideways.
Open in new window
Detach the screen and its action strip into a window of their own.
Why taps land where you click

The encoded video is slightly larger than the real screen — the phone pads the frame to a 16‑pixel grid. rPlayHub crops to the true screen so every click maps to the right point instead of drifting toward the corner.

Physical‑keyboard typing and the hardware Lock / Volume / Siri buttons are not sent yet. Everything the touchscreen can do is available today.

Screenshots & recording

Straight from the device, not a grab of the window.

Screenshot

Click the camera in the strip (or right‑click ▸ Screenshot). rPlayHub asks the device for a full‑resolution capture and saves it to your Desktop as rPlayHub‑<timestamp>.png, then reveals it in the Finder.

Recording

Click record to start; click again to stop. The clip is written by the engine as raw HEVC under its recordings/ folder, and the app reports the path when it finishes. Use rPlayHub ▸ Reveal Last Recording to find it.

One IDR per session

A recording started mid‑session may begin before a keyframe, so the first moment can look blocky until the picture refreshes. This is normal for this stream.

Device information

The Info tab — what the device says about itself.

Name, model, iOS build, ECID, serial number, battery level, and storage. It reads over the same channel Finder and Xcode use, so it keeps answering even while another tool — including Apple's Device Hub — currently holds the device.

Find an identifier fast

Right‑click a device in the sidebar to copy its UDID without opening a session.

Apps

The Apps tab — what's installed, and start / stop.

  • List. User‑installed apps and Apple's own, the same set Device Hub shows. Internal and hidden apps stay out.
  • Launch. Double‑click an app, or use Launch, to bring it to the foreground.
  • Terminate. Stop a running app; find its process first if you need the identifier.

Console

The Console tab — the device's live system log.

A running stream of the phone's syslog, with a filter field to narrow it to the process or text you care about. It opens its own connection so a never‑ending stream never blocks a tap or a screenshot.

Files & crash reports

The Files tab — the Media partition and crash logs, over AFC.

  • Browse. Double‑click a folder to enter it.
  • Pull a file. Double‑click a file to copy it to ~/Downloads/rPlayHub and reveal it.
  • Export All. Copies every crash report to ~/Downloads/rPlayHub/crash‑reports — the phone's crash mover is nudged first so fresh reports appear.

Profiles

The Profiles tab — provisioning and configuration profiles.

Provisioning profiles (name, app‑ID, team, UUID, expiry, and registered devices) and the configuration profiles installed on the device (identifier, name, organization). This is Device Hub's third inspector tab.

Power actions

Restart, Shut Down, Sleep — each confirms first.

Available from the action strip and both right‑click menus. Each asks for confirmation before it acts.

Restart
Reboots the phone. The connection drops and the app reconnects on its own after about 45 seconds.
Shut Down
Powers the phone off; it stays off until you press its side button.
Sleep
Turns the display off, as the side button would.

Simulators

The simulators you actually use, listed alongside physical devices.

rPlayHub lists the Simulators installed on this Mac — filtered to the ones you've used, matching Device Hub — and can boot, shut down, and screenshot them. Live mirroring and input are for physical devices only: a simulator isn't a display on your Mac, so there's no stream to show.

Troubleshooting

The handful of things that come up first.

The title says it's waiting for the engine

The app retries the connection every couple of seconds and shows what it's waiting for in the window title, so start order doesn't matter. If it never connects, confirm the background service was approved in System Settings ▸ General ▸ Login Items & Extensions.

The screen is there, but clicks do nothing

Input rides a different channel than the picture. Make sure a physical device is selected (simulators don't take taps) and that the session is live — the action strip appears under the screen only when it is.

No picture on a phone that just rebooted

Unlock the phone. The Developer Disk Image can't be mounted while it's locked, and without it there's no video. rPlayHub mounts it automatically once you unlock.

A device is greyed out or missing

Reconnect the cable or bring it onto the same Wi‑Fi network, confirm it's paired and trusted, and that Developer Mode is on. Use rPlayHub ▸ Reconnect to rescan.

If the picture freezes but everything reads "connected"

If the phone leaves Wi‑Fi mid‑session the video can stall. Reconnect the device or switch away and back to it in the sidebar to start a fresh session.
